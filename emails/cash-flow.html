Cash Flow Playbook

Growth doesn't stall from
low sales. It stalls from cash.

You can have record sales and still run out of money to reorder. These four habits keep cash moving so growth never has to wait on a payout.

4 ways to keep cash flowing

Cash flow problems rarely look like a lack of sales. They look like inventory you can't afford to reorder. Here's the concept behind each lever, and exactly what to do about it.

01  ·  KNOW YOUR PAYOUT SCHEDULE

Cash isn't real until Amazon releases it

THE CONCEPT

Amazon holds your sales revenue and releases it on a fixed schedule, typically every 14 days, minus fees, refunds, and reserves. Until that payout actually hits your bank, that money doesn't exist for planning purposes.

THE MOVE

Map your payout dates against your bill due dates and inventory orders. Knowing exactly when cash lands lets you time purchases deliberately, instead of spending against sales you haven't been paid for yet.

02  ·  BORROW FOR FREE, TEMPORARILY

Let the bank fund your growth, not your margin

THE CONCEPT

Many business credit cards and lines of credit offer 0% or very low interest for the first 12 to 18 months. That's a real window of free capital, if you use it on purpose.

THE MOVE

Use a 0% intro card to bridge inventory purchases while your cash is tied up in Amazon's payout cycle, then pay it down before the promotional rate ends. Treat the introductory period like a deadline, not a permanent line.

03  ·  BUY NOW, PAY LATER (LEGITIMATELY)

Ask your dealer for net terms

THE CONCEPT

Net terms (net 30, 60, even 90) let you receive inventory now and pay for it later, once you've had time to sell through it and collect the payout. Many dealers offer this, but usually only to sellers who ask.

THE MOVE

Once you've built a track record with a supplier, ask directly about net terms. It's one of the simplest ways to buy more inventory without tying up cash you don't have yet.

04  ·  TURN INVENTORY FASTER

Speed is a cash flow strategy

THE CONCEPT

Every day your inventory sits between your supplier and an Amazon warehouse is a day your cash is frozen. Faster delivery means faster sell-through, faster payouts, and faster reordering.

THE MOVE

Tighten the handoff between your supplier, your prep center, and Amazon. Fewer delays receiving, prepping, and shipping in directly means more inventory turns per year, without raising a dollar of new capital.
