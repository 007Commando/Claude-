Stefano's brief, pasted 2026-09-28. Source of truth for the copy; the
metadata record lives in `emails.csv`, the build plan in `03-plan.md`.

## The philosophy

These emails should not feel like an automation.

Across the sequence, the reader should experience:

**Direct response** — bold hooks, curiosity, urgency, entertainment.

**Founder field notes** — Stefano explaining something he has learned from actually operating in Amazon/e-commerce.

**Education** — useful information they can implement without purchasing anything.

**Proof** — Trustpilot reviews, customer screenshots, real outcomes and real numbers.

**Demonstration** — GIFs showing Apex doing something that would otherwise take substantial manual work.

**Objection handling** — acknowledging why someone hasn't started instead of pretending the objection doesn't exist.

**Paradigm shifts** — challenging assumptions like "I need more suppliers."

**Seasonality** — Q4, Prime events, holiday inventory, January planning, etc., only when genuinely relevant.

**Short founder emails** — 50–150 words that feel like Stefano personally sent them.

**Long-form emails** — 500–1,000 words when the story warrants it.

The objective is unpredictability.

The reader should never think:

"Here comes another Apex feature email."

After 30–60 days, don't simply ask which **email** won.

Ask which **mechanism** won.

For example:

Do proof emails generate fewer clicks but substantially more trials?

Do GIF emails outperform screenshots?

Do 80-word founder emails beat 700-word direct-response emails?

Does educational content produce delayed conversions?

Do numerical subject lines increase opens but hurt trial-start quality?

Does the seller segment respond more strongly to operational pain than supplier access?

Does the beginner segment respond more strongly to clarity than income opportunity?

That's how this campaign becomes smarter over time instead of simply replacing "bad emails" with new guesses.
