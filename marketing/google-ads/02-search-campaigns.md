# Search campaigns, one by one

1,314 keywords across 9 campaigns and 47 ad groups. The CSVs are the source of
truth; this file is why they look the way they do.

All campaigns: United States only, English, **Search Network only, search
partners OFF, Display expansion OFF**. Display expansion on a search campaign
is how a $12 budget becomes 4,000 worthless impressions on a recipe blog.

---

## Match types

**Conquest campaigns use exact and phrase only. Never broad.** A broad-match
keyword on a rival's name will fetch "aura ring", "jungle scout badge
requirements" and "helium balloon delivery", and each of those clicks costs the
same as a real one.

Phrase match is added only on the five shapes where the tail genuinely varies:
`alternative`, `alternatives`, `alternative to`, `competitors`, `vs`. People
write "sellerboard alternative for wholesale sellers", and exact match would
miss it.

Category campaigns get exact and phrase. A broad-match test ad group is worth
adding in month two, gated behind Smart Bidding, never on manual CPC.

## S01 | Brand Defence, $5/day

Three ad groups: Core, Modules (Apex Gold, Green, Blue, Red, Black) and
Programmes (Purchase Order Program, Apex Elite, PrimeWell).

Worth saying plainly: brand campaigns mostly buy clicks you would have had for
free. This one exists for one reason. The moment we start bidding on
"sellerboard alternative", the people who own those tools get a report showing
Apex as a competitor in their auction, and some of them will bid back. $5/day
holds the top of our own brand page against that.

The negative list here is unusually long because "apex" is one of the most
contested words in English: Apex Legends, Apex Trader Funding, Apex Learning,
Apex Tool Group, Apex Systems, Apex Hosting. All excluded.

Watch: if impression share lost to rank on `[apex applications]` goes above 10%,
raise the bid. Brand CPC should sit near the floor.

## S02 | Conquest, Repricers, $12/day

Aura, SellerSnap, BQool, Informed Repricer, Repricer.com.

This is the strongest switch story in the account, and it is not "we are
cheaper". Aura runs $47 to $297 and BQool $25 to $300 for repricing alone.
Apex is $149 for the whole loop with the repricer included in every plan. The
argument in the ad is **"repricing is one step"**, and the argument on the
landing page is the workflow-coverage table that shows four of five steps
filled on our side and one on theirs.

The second argument is sharper and more technical: **their floor is a number
you type, ours is your landed cost.** A seller who has ever set a min price by
hand and watched it eat a margin understands that sentence immediately.

**Aura needs care.** The word "aura" alone belongs to photo frames, sleep
rings, crystals and wellness. Every Aura keyword is therefore built from
`goaura`, `go aura` or `aura repricer`, never bare `aura`, and the conquest
negative list blocks aura frames, rings, photography, colours and cleansing.
`Informed` and `Repricer.com` get the same treatment for the same reason.

## S03 | Conquest, Sourcing & Ops, $12/day

SellerAmp, Third Party Profits, Scan Unlimited, Rocket Source, Seller
Assistant, Tactical Arbitrage, Boxem, 2D Workflow.

SellerAmp is probably the highest-volume rival name in this account, and it is
also the one where we should be most careful in the copy. SellerAmp is a
scan-one-item-at-a-time tool: a mobile app and a browser extension. Apex does
not replicate either. Our own comparison page says so. So the ad does not claim
to replace it; it makes the other argument, **"stop scanning one by one"**, and
lets the seller decide whether a whole supplier price list is how they actually
buy.

**Two ad groups stay paused and cannot be unpaused as shipped:** Third Party
Profits and Tactical Arbitrage have no page under `/compare/`, so they point at
the index, which does not answer a comparative question. Either write those two
comparison pages, or delete the ad groups. Sending conquest traffic to an index
is paying a $4 click to make someone choose from a menu.

Third Party Profits is worth the page. It was on the list of names to target
first, and it sits in exactly our lane: a wholesale-sourcing offer rather than
a general research tool.

## S04 | Conquest, Analytics & Research, $11/day

sellerboard, SmartScout, Helium 10, Jungle Scout, InventoryLab / Seller 365.

Highest search volume, weakest switch logic, so it gets the smallest conquest
budget of the three. Somebody searching "sellerboard alternative" wants cheaper
profit analytics; sellerboard is about $15 a month presented annually and we are
$149. We will not win that on price and must not try.

The argument is **"a dashboard that leads somewhere"**: the same numbers that
show the margin build the purchase order and set the repricer floor. It is a
real difference and it is also a smaller audience, which is what the budget
reflects.

Helium 10 and Jungle Scout are mostly private-label audiences. Expect poor
conversion rates and be ready to pause them after 200 clicks. They are in the
account to be measured, not because they are assumed to work.

## S05 | Category, Repricing, $14/day

Four ad groups: Core, Buy Box, Profit Floors, Wholesale.

The single highest-intent non-brand set and the biggest budget. `Profit Floors`
is the ad group to watch: "repricer with profit floor", "break even repricer
amazon", "repricer that uses cost of goods". Low volume, and the closest match
between what someone typed and what Apex Gold actually is.

Negatives exclude eBay, Walmart, Shopify and Etsy repricing, plus airline and
hotel dynamic pricing, plus the definitional queries ("what is repricing")
that are reading, not buying.

## S06 | Category, Wholesale Scanning, $12/day

Four ad groups: Price Lists, Bulk Lookup, Wholesale Software, Ungating.

The truest description of the product. "Supplier price list scanner", "scan
supplier catalog amazon", "amazon wholesale software" are what Apex Green is,
with no translation needed.

`Ungating` is here rather than in S08 because "amazon ungating checker" is a
software search, while "how to get ungated on amazon" is a how-to search. They
look adjacent and convert completely differently.

Negatives are heavy on the wrong kind of scanner (barcode hardware, document,
virus, network, port) and the wrong kind of seller (retail arbitrage, online
arbitrage, dropshipping, private label, liquidation pallets).

## S07 | Category, Profit & Analytics, $9/day

Four ad groups: Tracker, True Cost, Restock, Review Requests.

Crowded and cheap-looking, which is the trap. Half the traffic on "amazon
seller profit tracker" is people who want free, and the other half already own
sellerboard. Negatives exclude QuickBooks, Xero, bookkeeping and tax, plus the
Amazon-the-stock queries that pollute anything containing "amazon profit".

`Restock` points at `/amazon-inventory-management-software` and `Review
Requests` at `/amazon-review-automation`, because both pages already exist and
both answer their query directly.

## S08 | Category, Suppliers & Ungating, $10/day

Four ad groups: Distributors, Ungating, Prep Centers, First Order.

Top of funnel, and judged differently from everything else in the account.
These searchers are not shopping for software; they are trying to find stock.
The offer is the **free account with three vetted US distributors on sign up and
three more every month**, which is confirmed true and is a genuinely strong
answer to "amazon wholesale suppliers".

Judge this campaign on free signups, not trials. If it is held to a $100 trial
CPA it will look like a failure and be switched off, when what it is actually
doing is filling the top of the funnel that the lifecycle emails work on.

`Prep Centers` points at `/prep-center-network`, which is a directory. It will
attract sellers looking for a prep centre rather than software, which is the
point: they are wholesale sellers, and the directory is a real answer.

## S10 | DSA, Comparisons & Blog, $5/day

Two ad groups, targeting `URL_CONTAINS /compare` and `URL_CONTAINS /blog`.

This is a query miner, not a scale lever. The comparison pages cover sixteen
rivals with real pricing, which means Google can match them to "X vs Y" queries
nobody thought to add as a keyword. Every week, read its search terms report and
promote the good ones into S02 to S04 as exact-match keywords, then add them as
DSA negatives so they do not compete with themselves.

**Never point a DSA at the whole site.** It would generate ads for `/auth`,
`/checkout` and `/terms`, and write its own headlines for them.

## Negative keyword architecture

818 negative rows, in four layers:

1. **Universal**, on every campaign: free, cracked, torrent, coupon, AppSumo,
   jobs, salary, login, Wikipedia, Glassdoor. People who will never pay.
2. **Conquest-specific**: the words that turn a rival's name into something
   else (aura frames, boy scouts), plus tutorial, user guide, refund, cancel
   subscription, affiliate program, reddit. Existing customers of theirs who
   are not switching.
3. **Category-specific**: the wrong vertical for each set, listed above.
4. **Cross-campaign routing**: every non-brand campaign negatives out
   "apex applications", and every non-conquest campaign negatives out every
   rival name. Without this the cheap category campaign quietly answers
   conquest queries, wins them on a lower bid, and both reports become fiction.

Layer 4 is the one people skip and it is the one that decides whether the
numbers in this account mean anything.

The one thing not automatable: **read the search terms report every week for the
first month.** Ten minutes of that beats any negative list written in advance,
including this one.
